Notation

Use <…> for a component, with 0 and 1 inside. Both the domain and codomain may be snake dens.

L is a loop (an east step); C is a coloop (a north step). They may appear inside or outside brackets in f and g, but not in c.

Angle brackets ⟨ ⟩, parentheses, and x y also work. Spaces are ignored. A bare binary codomain such as 010 means ⟨010⟩. Use ε for an empty word or den.

Each slither has one letter for every letter in c. Deleting L and C from both must give the same domain a. Up to 160 letters in c.

Random pairs

Random facet reductions choose f. Allowed moves then choose a distinct g with the same pruning in each codomain component. The generated pair always has a move path. Sampling is not uniform.

Moves stay within each component of c. The two prunings in every row must agree for a path to exist.

Method

For a single snake, move f and g to leftmost representatives, match their bound tails, then reverse the moves for g. Closed excursions are removed. The route need not be shortest.

For a snake-den codomain, this is applied separately in each component. The pruning in each codomain component is preserved by the allowed moves. When those prunings differ, both diagrams are shown and the mismatch is explained instead of claiming a path.

Every move and its ambient witness are checked inside c. The diagrams show a discrete chain of combinatorial moves, not the parameter of an algebraic homotopy.
